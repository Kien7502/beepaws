# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Pet owners (dog and cat) who want to handle more of their pet's routine care at home:
dental, and later grooming. They are cost-aware and a little skeptical: they have been
burned by hype, by devices that do not do what the box promised, and by a vet or groomer
bill that felt avoidable. They browse on phone and desktop, usually deciding whether an
at-home device is actually trustworthy and made for *their* specific pet (a small breed,
a senior, an anxious dog, a cat whose mouth has been quietly ignored).

The job to be done: decide, without feeling sold to, that this product is real, that it
was made for a pet like theirs, and that the company will not pretend to be more than it is.

## Product Purpose

BeePaws sells at-home pet wellness tools. The launch product is dental (an at-home scaler);
grooming is a later chapter, gated behind a single `SHOW_GROOMING` flag, never teased as
"coming soon". The storefront's job is to earn trust for at-home care and convert a skeptic
honestly, one product at a time. Success is a wary owner thinking "this was built for the pet
I actually have" and buying without the aftertaste of having been hyped.

## Brand Personality

Honest, warm, grounded. The voice states what the product literally does, owns its limits
(it does not replace your vet), and hands the decision to the owner ("she decides"). It never
shouts, never invents a statistic or a review, never uses the supercharge/transform/game-changer
register. Warmth is real but earned: it should come from the pets on the page, the plain-spoken
copy, and the pacing, not from a decorative coat of "cozy".

Emotional goal: calm confidence. The reader should feel respected, not worked.

## Anti-references

- **Bright DTC pet-store** (Chewy-bright primaries, cartoon mascots, confetti energy). Wrong tone.
- **Hype/urgency marketing** (countdown timers, "revolutionary", exclamation copy, fake scarcity).
- **Invented proof** (stock-photo pets, made-up star counts, "vet-approved" badges, testimonials
  we did not receive). This is a trust brand; fabricated evidence is the one unforgivable move.
- **The templated AI-landing look**: cream body + reflex serif + a tiny uppercase eyebrow over
  every section + identical icon-heading-text card grids + one uniform fade-up on every section.
  This is the specific trap this visual pass exists to climb out of.

## Design Principles

1. **Honest by construction.** Real pets, real names, honest hedges. Evidentiary imagery (proof,
   founder) is real or absent, never stock. Illustrative imagery may be generated but must be
   quality-inspected. Never manufacture trust.
2. **Made for the pet you actually have.** Specificity beats generic warmth. Small breeds, seniors,
   anxious dogs, ignored cats: name them.
3. **Owner agency.** Inform and let the owner decide. Guide, do not push. No dark patterns.
4. **Calm confidence over hype.** Quiet, grounded, declarative. The design can be bold in
   composition and imagery while the voice stays level.
5. **Warmth from craft, not defaults.** The brand's warmth must be carried by imagery, typography,
   motion, and pacing, so the palette can stop doing all the work and stop reading as templated.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA: body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1, placeholders held to body contrast (no light-gray
"elegant" body on tinted cream). Reduced motion is fully honored (already wired through the
`.ds-*` system with fade-only fallbacks). Keyboard and focus states legible on every warm
background. Alt text written in the brand voice, describing the real pet and moment, not "dog".
